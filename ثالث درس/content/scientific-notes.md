## 1. SRT-Derived Scientific Content (Source Material)

The core concepts below were directly extracted from the primary transcript/SRT material (`الدرس.txt`):

1. **Definition of Movement (مفهوم الحركة):**
   - Movement is an inherent biological activity driven self-initiatively in response to internal stimuli (e.g., hunger) or external stimuli (e.g., heat, cold, touch, light, gravity, moisture).
   - **Response Direction:**
     - *Positive response (استجابة إيجابية):* Growth or movement toward the stimulus.
     - *Negative response (استجابة سلبية):* Growth or movement away from the stimulus.

2. **Classification of Movement by Moving Part (تصنيف الحركة حسب الجزء المتحرك):**
   - **Continuous Movement (حركة دائبة):** Internal cellular movement required for vital metabolic activities and transport across organelles (Example: Cytoplasmic Streaming / الحركة الدورانية السيتوبلازمية).
   - **Positional Movement (حركة موضعية):** Movement of an organ or body part relative to the rest of the fixed body (Examples: Peristalsis / الحركة الدودية للأمعاء, limb movement, heart pulsation, Mimosa drooping).
   - **Locomotion (حركة كلية):** Movement of the organism as a whole from one place to another to seek food, mate/reproduce, or avoid environmental hazards (volcanoes, earthquakes).
   - **Unified Example (*Amoeba*):** *Amoeba* exhibits all three types: cytoplasmic streaming (continuous), pseudopodia extension (positional), and moving toward bacteria/food (locomotion).

3. **Plant Movement Types (حركات النبات):**
   - **Touch Movement (حركة اللمس):** Exemplified by *Mimosa pudica*. Tapping leaflets causes them to collapse and droop.
   - **Sleep & Wakefulness (حركة النوم واليقظة):** Exhibited by *Mimosa* and some legumes. Leaflets fold/close at night (sleep) and expand during daylight (wakefulness).
   - **Mechanism of Touch & Sleep Movements:** Driven by osmotic water movement in leaf pulvini (انتفاخات المحاور والوريقات) which act as biological joints. Turgor pressure loss in the lower, more sensitive half of the pulvinus causes water exit and leaf drooping.
   - **Tropism (الانتحاء):** Curvature of plant organs when exposed unevenly to a directional unilateral stimulus.
     - *Phototropism (الانتحاء الضوئي):* Stimulus = Unilateral Light. Stem = Positive (+), Root = Negative (-).
     - *Geotropism (الانتحاء الأرضي):* Stimulus = Gravity. Horizontal placement: Stem = Negative (-), Root = Positive (+).
     - *Hydrotropism (الانتحاء المائي):* Stimulus = Moisture/Water. Root = Positive (+).
   - **Boysen-Jensen Experiment (تجربة بويسون جونسون):** Proved the oat coleoptile tip (*Avena sativa*) secretes chemical messengers (Auxins):
     - *Experiment A (Tip cut):* Removal of tip stops phototropic curvature.
     - *Experiment B (Gelatin block inserted):* Phototropic curvature resumes (Auxins diffuse through gelatin).
     - *Experiment C (Mica plate inserted):* Phototropic curvature blocked (Mica blocks Auxin diffusion).
   - **Auxin Mechanism in Phototropism:** Unilateral light causes Auxins to migrate to the shaded side. Higher Auxin concentration on the shaded side causes greater cell elongation in the stem than on the illuminated side, bending the stem toward light.
   - **Tendril Pulling Movement (حركة الشد بالمحاليق):**
     - Occurs in climbing plants with weak stems (Grapevine / العنب, Pea / البازلاء, Ivy / اللبلاب, Luffa / اللوف).
     - Sequence: Weak Stem → Tendril Searches → Touch Support → Coiling → Attachment → Stem Pulling & Upright Support.
     - Differential Growth Mechanism: Contact side grows slower due to Auxin migration away; non-contact side grows faster, causing the tendril to coil tightly around the support.

## 2. Curriculum-Completion Additions (Egyptian 3rd Secondary Biology Syllabus)

To satisfy full national secondary biology curriculum requirements beyond the raw SRT transcript, the following standard curricular components are explicitly integrated:

1. **Contractile Roots (الجذور الشادة):**
   - Exemplified by Narcissus bulbs (*Crinum* / *Narcissus*) and corms (*Colocasia*).
   - **Mechanism:** Root contraction pulls the corm or bulb vertically downward into the soil until it reaches a suitable depth.
   - **Biological Purpose:** Ensures firm anchorage against wind and environmental threats while protecting aerial parts and underground storage organs.

2. **Tendril Failure Consequence (ماذا يحدث إذا لم يجد الحالق الدعامة):**
   - If a tendril fails to find a suitable solid support during its aerial rotation, it wilts, dies, and falls off.

3. **Auxin Polarity in Roots vs Stems:**
   - While high Auxin concentrations stimulate cell elongation in stems, high Auxin concentrations inhibit cell elongation in roots (root cells are far more sensitive to Auxin). This explains why roots bend away from light (negative phototropism) and toward gravity (positive geotropism).

## 3. Scientific Assumptions & Visual Simplifications

To ensure full transparency and avoid misleading quantitative claims in visual simulations:

1. **Auxin Concentration Engine (Section 06):**
   - *Visualization:* Uses a qualitative gradient bar (`LOW` → `MEDIUM` → `HIGH`) rather than arbitrary numerical percentage figures (such as 65%/35%).
   - *Educational Goal:* To visually demonstrate that unequal Auxin distribution induces unequal cellular elongation, leading to tissue curvature.

2. **Pulvinus Turgor Model (Section 04):**
   - *Visualization:* Depicts osmotic water molecules exiting lower pulvinus cells into intercellular spaces / upper cells.
   - *Educational Goal:* To illustrate how turgor loss mimics joint relaxation without presenting a detailed molecular ion-channel physics simulation.

3. **Tendril Coiling Stage (Section 08):**
   - *Visualization:* Step-by-step 5-stage mechanical progression (Weak stem → Search → Contact → Differential Growth & Coiling → Upright pulling).
   - *Educational Goal:* Clearly distinguishes the slower growth rate of the contact side from the faster growth rate of the non-contact side.
